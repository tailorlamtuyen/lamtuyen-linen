Before You Start — Read This First

📏
Use a soft flexible tape measure — not a stiff metal ruler. A sewing tape is ideal and inexpensive at any market.
👕
Wear form-fitting clothing or just underwear. Baggy clothes will add unwanted centimetres to every measurement.
🧍
Stand in your natural posture. Feet together, arms relaxed at sides. Don't breathe in or push out — just be normal.
🔄
Measure twice, record once. If two readings differ by more than 1cm, take a third and use the middle value.
📐
Keep the tape parallel to the floor for all horizontal measurements (chest, waist, hips). Don't let it slope.
💬
When in doubt, contact us. We'd rather guide you through it than remake your garment. 0935 023 757.
Section 01

Upper Body
Measurements

These measurements shape the fit across your shoulders, chest, and upper back — the foundation of any well-made garment.

01 Chest / Bust
Used for all tops, dresses, jackets, and suits. The most important single measurement.
Step-by-Step Instructions
1
Wrap the tape measure horizontally around the fullest part of your chest, directly under your armpits and across your shoulder blades at the back.
2
For women: measure at the fullest point of the bust. Keep the tape parallel to the floor all the way around.
3
The tape should be snug but not tight — you should be able to slip one finger under it. Don't breathe in.
4
Note the measurement where the tape end meets the tape. Record in cm.
Tip: If you're between sizes, give us both your chest and your natural waist — we'll grade the pattern to fit both zones perfectly.
cm
02 Shoulder Width
Determines where your sleeve begins. Critical for jackets, blouses, and all tops.
Step-by-Step Instructions
1
Stand with your arms relaxed at your sides.
2
Locate the bony tip of each shoulder (the acromion point) — this is where your arm socket meets your shoulder.
3
Measure from tip to tip across the back in a straight line (not following the curve of your neck).
4
A helper makes this easier. If measuring alone, use a mirror to check the tape lies flat across your upper back.
Tip: If you're unsure of the exact shoulder point, try gently rolling your arm — the bony bump that moves is it.
cm
03 Neck Circumference
Used for shirts, collars, and any garment with a close neckline.
Step-by-Step Instructions
1
Wrap the tape around the base of your neck, where a shirt collar would sit.
2
Add 1–2cm ease so the collar isn't choking you. The tape should feel comfortable, like a loosely closed collar.
3
Record the measurement at the front where the tape ends meet.
Tip: If between sizes, go with the larger measurement — a collar can always be taken in; a tight one cannot be let out.
cm
Section 02

Torso & Waist
Measurements

These measurements shape the middle of the body and ensure garments sit correctly at the natural waist, hips, and crotch.

04 Natural Waist
The narrowest part of your torso. Defines where all waistbands, waist seams, and dresses nip in.
Step-by-Step Instructions
1
Stand relaxed. Bend slightly to one side — the crease that forms is your natural waist.
2
Wrap the tape around this crease, keeping it parallel to the floor all the way around.
3
The tape should be comfortably snug — you can fit one finger underneath but it's not loose.
4
Do NOT suck in your stomach. Breathe normally and measure at the end of a natural exhale.
Tip: Your natural waist is usually 2–3cm above your belly button, not at belt level.
cm
05 Hip Circumference
Used for all bottoms, dresses, and any garment below the waist. Essential for skirts and trousers.
Step-by-Step Instructions
1
Stand with your feet together. The hip measurement is taken at the fullest part of your hips and buttocks.
2
This is typically 18–23cm below your natural waist, at the widest point of your hips/seat.
3
Wrap the tape all the way around, keeping it parallel to the floor. Make sure it passes over the fullest part of both hips.
4
Keep it snug but not tight. Don't pull the tape — it should sit against your body without pressing in.
Tip: Check in a mirror that the tape is level all the way around — it often dips at the back.
cm
06 Crotch / Rise
Front waistband to back waistband passing between the legs. Critical for all trousers, shorts, and fitted skirts.
Step-by-Step Instructions
1
Method A (sitting): Sit on a hard, flat chair. Measure from your natural waist down the side of your body to the seat of the chair. This gives your total rise.
2
Method B (standing): Hold one end of the tape at your front waistband centre. Pass it down through your legs and up to the centre back waistband. Record the total.
3
Also record your front rise (from waist centre front to crotch seam) and back rise (from crotch seam to waist centre back) separately if you can.
Tip: Rise affects comfort significantly. If you have a longer torso, tell us — we'll adjust the trouser rise so it never pulls or gaps.
cm
07 Torso / Back Length
From the top of the shoulder to the natural waist. Positions waist seams correctly on all dresses, blouses, and jackets.
Step-by-Step Instructions
1
Stand straight. Place one end of the tape at the highest point of your shoulder (at the shoulder seam point).
2
Run the tape straight down your back to your natural waist line (where you bend sideways).
3
Keep the tape flat against your back — don't let it swing around the side.
Tip: This is especially important for fitted dresses. Different from your total height.
cm
Section 03

Arm
Measurements

Sleeve length, bicep, tricep, and wrist measurements ensure your sleeves fit beautifully whether loose and flowing or fitted.

08 Sleeve Length
Determines how long your sleeves are. Give us both your full-length and preferred short-sleeve length if ordering a shirt.
Step-by-Step Instructions
1
For long sleeve: Bend your elbow slightly (about 45°). Measure from the tip of your shoulder (shoulder point), along the outside of your arm, over the elbow bend, down to your wrist bone.
2
For short sleeve: Measure from the shoulder tip to wherever you'd like the sleeve to end — mid upper arm, elbow, or forearm.
3
Tell us your preferred sleeve end point in your style notes.
Tip: Linen sleeves will drape. Always measure with your arm slightly bent — a straight arm gives a sleeve that's too short when you reach forward.
cm
09 Bicep Circumference
Used to size sleeve openings. Prevents tight sleeves that restrict movement or bunch at the arm.
Step-by-Step Instructions
1
Let your arm hang relaxed at your side. Do not flex.
2
Wrap the tape around the thickest part of your upper arm — usually about one-third of the way from shoulder to elbow.
3
Keep the tape snug but comfortable — you shouldn't feel pressure.
Tip: For fitted sleeves we add 3–4cm ease. If you prefer a looser sleeve, note that in your style instructions.
cm
10 Tricep / Mid-Arm
The circumference at the mid-upper arm (back of arm). Important for fitted sleeves that need to move with you.
Step-by-Step Instructions
1
Arm relaxed at your side.
2
Measure around the midpoint of your upper arm — halfway between shoulder tip and elbow point.
3
This is similar to bicep but positioned slightly lower and will be a slightly different measurement for most people.
Tip: This measurement primarily affects flutter and wide sleeves. If ordering a loose blouse, a rough estimate is fine.
cm
11 Wrist Circumference
Used for cuffs and tight long sleeves. Ensures buttons close comfortably.
Step-by-Step Instructions
1
Measure around the wrist joint — where your wrist bends, just below the wrist bone.
2
Keep the tape snug but not tight. You should be able to move your wrist comfortably.
Tip: For loose or button cuffs, we add 2–3cm ease automatically.
cm
Section 04

Lower Body
Measurements

For trousers, skirts, and all garments from the waist down. These ensure the perfect fit from hip to hem.

12 Inseam Length
The inside leg measurement. Determines trouser length and hem placement.
Step-by-Step Instructions
1
Stand with your feet about shoulder-width apart.
2
Place one end of the tape at your crotch seam (where a trouser seam would sit between your legs).
3
Run the tape down the inside of your leg to your ankle bone.
4
Also tell us your preferred hem level: ankle, just above ankle, floor-length, or a specific cm from the floor.
Tip: It's easier with a friend. If measuring alone, a well-fitting pair of trousers you already own gives a reliable inseam measurement.
cm
13 Thigh Circumference
The widest part of your upper leg. Critical for trousers — ensures you can sit and move without pulling.
Step-by-Step Instructions
1
Stand with your feet hip-width apart, weight evenly distributed.
2
Wrap the tape around the fullest part of your upper thigh — usually about 3–5cm below the crotch.
3
Keep the tape parallel to the floor. Snug but not compressing.
Tip: Don't forget to measure — many people skip this and end up with trousers that fit the waist but are too tight to sit in.
cm
14 Calf Circumference
The widest part of the lower leg. Important for cropped trousers, fitted pants, and any leg that tapers below the knee.
Step-by-Step Instructions
1
Stand with weight on both feet.
2
Wrap the tape around the fullest part of your calf muscle — usually about midway between knee and ankle.
3
Keep tape parallel to the floor. Don't tense your calf — stand relaxed.
Tip: Required only for tapered, cropped, or fitted leg styles. For wide-leg linen trousers, this is less critical.
cm
15 Ankle Circumference
Used for tapered trousers and any hem that narrows toward the ankle. Ensures the hem slides on and off comfortably.
Step-by-Step Instructions
1
Measure around the narrowest part of your ankle — just above the ankle bone joint.
2
Keep the tape snug. This measurement ensures tapered hems don't need to be stepped into — they can be pulled on.
Tip: For wide-leg or straight trousers, skip this one. Only needed for fitted, tapered, or cropped styles.
cm
16 Knee Circumference
Needed for mid-knee hems and trouser legs that fit closely around the knee — like tapered or cigarette trousers.
Step-by-Step Instructions
1
Stand with leg straight and relaxed.
2
Wrap the tape around the centre of the kneecap.
3
Keep the tape parallel to the floor. Snug but not tight.
Tip: Only relevant for fitted trousers. For wide-leg or relaxed styles, leave this blank.
cm
17 Outseam / Total Leg Length
From waist to desired hem along the outside of the leg. Used alongside inseam to confirm trouser length proportions.
Step-by-Step Instructions
1
Stand straight. Measure from your natural waistline, down the outside of your leg, to the point where you want the hem.
2
For full-length: measure to the ankle bone or floor (specify which).
3
Note in your style description whether you want the hem to just graze the floor, sit at the ankle, or at another point.
Tip: Linen moves and breathes — floor-length linen pants are beautiful. Just specify your shoe heel height if applicable.
cm
Section 05

Additional
Measurements

Height, dress length, and overall garment length — these finish off the complete picture and ensure your garment is the exact right length for your body.

18 Total Height
Gives us your overall proportions. Used to proportion the entire garment to your frame, especially for dresses and sets.
Step-by-Step Instructions
1
Stand barefoot against a wall, feet together, standing as tall as naturally possible.
2
Place a flat book or ruler on top of your head, mark the wall, and measure from that mark to the floor.
3
Or simply step on a scale with a height measure — most record in cm.
Tip: Always give barefoot height. If you'll wear heels with the garment, tell us the heel height and we'll adjust the hem accordingly.
cm
19 Desired Garment Length
From the highest shoulder point to where you want the hem to fall. The most direct way to specify how long a dress or top should be.
Step-by-Step Instructions
1
Place one end of the tape at your highest shoulder point.
2
Let the tape fall straight down the front of your body to the point where you want the hem.
3
Common references: mini = 50–60cm, midi = 90–105cm, maxi/floor = 130–145cm (varies by height).
Tip: If you have a dress you already love the length of, measure from its shoulder seam to the hem and give us that number.
cm
20 Back Width
From underarm seam to underarm seam across the widest part of the back. Prevents pulling across the back of jackets and blouses.
Step-by-Step Instructions
1
Stand naturally with arms relaxed.
2
Measure horizontally across your back from one armhole to the other, at the widest point of the upper back (below the shoulder blades).
3
This is about halfway between your shoulder and natural waist on your back.
Tip: A helper is very useful here. If measuring alone, measure a well-fitting shirt across the back and use that figure.
cm
