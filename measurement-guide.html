Before You Start

📏
Use a soft flexible tape measure — not a stiff ruler. Available at any market for a few thousand dong.
👕
Wear form-fitting clothing or underwear only. Baggy clothes add unwanted centimetres.
🧍
Stand in your natural posture. Feet together, arms relaxed. Breathe normally.
🔄
Measure twice, record once. If two readings differ by more than 1cm, take a third.
📐
Keep tape parallel to the floor for all horizontal measurements.
💬
When in doubt, call us: 0935 023 757. We'd rather guide you than remake a garment.
Section 01

Upper Body
Measurements

01Chest / Bust
Most important measurement. Used for all tops, dresses, jackets.
How to Measure
1
Wrap tape horizontally around the fullest part of your chest, directly under your armpits.
2
For women: measure at the fullest point of the bust. Keep tape parallel to floor.
3
Tape should be snug but not tight. Don't breathe in.
Tip: One finger should fit comfortably under the tape.
cm
02Shoulder Width
Determines where your sleeve begins. Critical for all tops and jackets.
How to Measure
1
Stand with arms relaxed. Find the bony tip of each shoulder (where your arm socket meets your shoulder).
2
Measure from tip to tip across the back in a straight line.
Tip: A helper makes this much easier. If alone, measure a well-fitting top from seam to seam across the upper back.
cm
03Neck Circumference
Used for shirt collars and close necklines.
How to Measure
1
Wrap tape around the base of your neck where a shirt collar would sit.
2
Add 1–2cm ease so the collar isn't tight.
Tip: If between sizes, go larger — a collar can be taken in but not let out.
cm
Section 02

Torso & Waist
Measurements

04Natural Waist
The narrowest part of your torso. Defines where waistbands and seams sit.
How to Measure
1
Bend slightly to one side — the crease that forms is your natural waist.
2
Wrap tape around this crease, parallel to the floor.
3
Do NOT suck in. Breathe normally and measure at the end of a natural exhale.
Tip: Your natural waist is usually 2–3cm above your belly button, not at belt level.
cm
05Hip Circumference
Used for all bottoms, dresses, and garments below the waist.
How to Measure
1
Stand with feet together. Measure at the fullest part of your hips and buttocks.
2
Usually 18–23cm below your natural waist.
3
Keep tape parallel to floor all the way around.
Tip: Check in a mirror — the tape often dips at the back.
cm
06Crotch / Rise
Front waistband to back waistband through the legs. Critical for all trousers.
How to Measure
1
Sit on a hard flat chair. Measure from your natural waist down the side of your body to the seat. This is your total rise.
2
Or standing: hold tape at front waistband centre, pass through legs, up to back waistband centre.
Tip: Rise affects comfort significantly. Tell us if you have a long or short torso.
cm
07Torso / Back Length
Shoulder to natural waist. Positions waist seams correctly on dresses and blouses.
How to Measure
1
Place one end at the highest point of your shoulder (shoulder seam point).
2
Run tape straight down your back to your natural waist line.
Tip: Especially important for fitted dresses.
cm
Section 03

Arm
Measurements

08Sleeve Length
How long your sleeves are. Give us both long and short sleeve preference.
How to Measure
1
Bend elbow slightly (45°). Measure from shoulder tip, along outside of arm, over elbow, down to wrist bone.
2
For short sleeve: measure from shoulder tip to where you want the sleeve to end.
Tip: Always measure with arm bent — a straight arm gives a sleeve that's too short when you reach.
cm
09Bicep Circumference
Sizes sleeve openings. Prevents tight sleeves that restrict movement.
How to Measure
1
Arm relaxed at your side. Do not flex.
2
Wrap tape around the thickest part of your upper arm.
Tip: For fitted sleeves we add 3–4cm ease automatically.
cm
10Tricep / Mid-Arm
Mid upper arm circumference. Important for fitted and flutter sleeves.
How to Measure
1
Arm relaxed. Measure around the midpoint of your upper arm — halfway between shoulder and elbow.
Tip: For loose blouses, a rough estimate is fine.
cm
11Wrist Circumference
Used for cuffs and long sleeve openings.
How to Measure
1
Measure around the wrist joint — just below the wrist bone. Snug but comfortable.
Tip: We add 2–3cm ease for button cuffs automatically.
cm
12Back Width
Armhole to armhole across upper back. Prevents pulling across the back of blouses.
How to Measure
1
Measure horizontally across back from one armhole to the other at the widest point of the upper back.
Tip: Measure a well-fitting shirt across the back for this one.
cm
Section 04

Lower Body
Measurements

13Inseam Length
Inside leg measurement. Determines trouser length and hem placement.
How to Measure
1
Stand feet shoulder-width apart. Place tape at crotch seam.
2
Run tape down inside of leg to ankle bone.
3
Tell us your preferred hem level in the style notes.
Tip: Measure a well-fitting pair of trousers from crotch seam to hem for an easy reference.
cm
14Thigh Circumference
Widest part of upper leg. Ensures trousers allow you to sit and move.
How to Measure
1
Stand feet hip-width apart. Wrap tape around fullest part of upper thigh, 3–5cm below the crotch.
Tip: Many people skip this — then can't sit in their trousers. Always measure it.
cm
15Calf Circumference
Widest part of lower leg. Important for tapered or cropped trousers.
How to Measure
1
Wrap tape around the fullest part of your calf muscle — midway between knee and ankle.
Tip: Only required for tapered styles. For wide-leg linen trousers, leave blank.
cm
16Knee Circumference
For mid-knee hems and fitted trouser legs.
How to Measure
1
Stand with leg straight and relaxed. Wrap tape around centre of kneecap.
Tip: Only needed for fitted/tapered styles. Leave blank for wide-leg.
cm
17Ankle Circumference
Ensures tapered hems can be pulled on comfortably.
How to Measure
1
Measure around the narrowest part of your ankle, just above the ankle bone. Snug but not tight.
Tip: Only needed for tapered or cropped styles.
cm
18Outseam / Total Leg Length
Waist to desired hem along outside of leg. Confirms trouser length.
How to Measure
1
From your natural waistline, measure down the outside of your leg to where you want the hem to fall.
Tip: Specify your heel height in the notes if ordering floor-length.
cm
Section 05

Height &
Garment Length

19Total Height
Gives us your overall proportions for the entire garment.
How to Measure
1
Stand barefoot against a wall. Place a flat book on top of your head, mark the wall, measure to the floor.
Tip: Always barefoot height. If you'll wear heels, note the heel height in style notes.
cm
20Desired Garment Length
From shoulder to where you want the hem to fall. The most direct way to specify dress length.
How to Measure
1
Tape from highest shoulder point straight down to where you want the hem.
2
Common: mini=50–60cm, midi=90–105cm, maxi/floor=130–145cm.
Tip: Measure a dress you already love the length of — shoulder seam to hem.
